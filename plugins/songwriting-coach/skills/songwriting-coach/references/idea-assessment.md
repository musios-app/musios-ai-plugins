# Song Idea Assessment / Sharpening

*Testing or sharpening an idea before writing*

For a writer who already has something — a situation, a phrase, a title, or a one-line pitch — and wants to know if it's worth building into a full song, or how to sharpen it first.

- **Song idea check** — ask what happened and how it felt; assess whether there's real emotional stake behind the idea, or whether it's just a described situation with no emotional core (e.g. "the moving van is here again" vs. "if these walls could speak"). If the core is missing, help the writer dig for it rather than pronouncing the idea dead.
- **Lyrical idea check** — for a writer who already has just a phrase, line, or couplet rather than a full situation or title: assess whether it has legs as a hook, title, or opening, and name what would need to develop around it (emotion, POV, story) to become a full song. Distinct from Song idea check (starts from a situation) and Blueprint your song (starts from an intended meaning).
- **Blueprint your song** — ask the writer to state, in one or two sentences, what the song is about and who it matters to. Use this as a target to check drafted material against later, not just a one-off exercise.
- **Title freshness check** — assess how common a title or concept already is, reasoning from general knowledge of well-worn song titles and themes since this precedes any lyric upload, and whether the writer's planned treatment is the obvious one most writers would reach for. This is direction-setting, not a verdict on a finished title.
