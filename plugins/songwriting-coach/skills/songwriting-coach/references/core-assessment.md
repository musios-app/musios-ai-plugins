# Core Assessment

*A first listen*

**Full core assessment** — what the writer gets by choosing "all" when running the Core Assessment (see Running an assessment). Runs all Core Assessment checks against the uploaded lyric and collates results into a single report; structured by song section in order, with the key points (the Report depth number, 5 at Full report) at the top; every section is listed; tools that find nothing to flag go on that section's ✅ line, check names first ("✅ Cliche, Singability: nothing flagged"); check names written in full throughout, one at a time, never shortened or combined (not "Arc", not "Beat / Sing")

- **Emotional arc review** — does the lyric build, resolve, or land as intended across the song?
- **Sliver of hope check** — checks whether a sad or heavy lyric includes at least one moment of hope, relief, or forward motion (often placed in the bridge) rather than remaining unrelieved despair from start to finish. This is a pattern worth flagging, not a rule — some songs are legitimately, deliberately hopeless throughout, and that can be the intended effect. If the lyric holds one negative emotion with no turn or lift anywhere, ask the writer whether that's the intended effect before treating it as a gap to fix.
- **Authenticity review** — does the lyric sound like a real person in a real moment, or does it feel constructed?
- **Cliche scan** — flag phrases, images and words listeners have heard many times before
- **Beat review** — assess vowel quality, stress placement, and syllable count against an implied beat or melody. If a genre is known (from Song mode & direction, or stated directly), calibrate against that genre's rhythmic norms rather than a generic standard — reggae's rhythmic placement, for instance, tolerates and even expects patterns a straight pop ballad wouldn't.
- **Singability review** — does the phrasing feel natural to perform, or does it fight a singer's breath and phrasing instincts? Read the lyric aloud, not just silently, as part of this check: reading aloud surfaces problems that don't show up on the page — lines that sound overly poetic or "written" rather than spoken, words that are physically hard to say in sequence, or a shift in register/voice that wasn't audible when just reading the text.

**Genre fit check** *(draft — held from the live Tool List until finalised)* — requires a stated genre (from Song mode & direction, from Song Idea Assessment / Sharpening, or just stated directly by the writer). Summarise the typical lyrical content of that genre — common subject matter, tropes, register, imagery conventions — then compare the draft against it, noting where it aligns with genre convention and where it diverges. Divergence is not automatically a problem: flag it and ask whether it's a deliberate choice (a fresh angle worth keeping) or an oversight (drifting out of the genre's expected territory without meaning to). If no genre has been stated anywhere in the conversation, ask before running this rather than guessing one.

**Emoji key:** 🟢 positive · 🔵 observation · 🟡 concern · 🔴 issue · ✅ nothing flagged

**Full core assessment protocol:**
1. Run all Core Assessment checks and present the report
2. Invite the writer to review, question, or push back on any finding — locked decisions, misreadings, and intentional choices are all fair grounds for revision
3. Update the report to reflect any agreed changes
4. Offer the finalised report as markdown or docx
