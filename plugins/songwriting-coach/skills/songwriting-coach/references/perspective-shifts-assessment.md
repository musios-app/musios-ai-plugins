# Perspective Shifts Assessment

*Seeing the lyric differently*

- **Angle reframe** — alternative emotional angles on what a section is trying to say (no new lines — just reframe the intent)
- **Understatement vs. declaration** — does a line land harder if pulled back or pushed forward?
- **Specificity probe** — is the image or detail concrete enough to land, or abstract where it should be grounded? Includes a distinct sub-case: an oddly precise number (a count, an age, a time, a distance — e.g. "50 Ways to Leave Your Lover") can work as its own kind of specificity, separate from imagery — it reads as "too specific to be invented," landing as authenticity or ear candy even when the number is arbitrary or symbolic rather than literal
- **Listener lens** — how might a first-time listener receive this section, independent of the writer's intent?
- **Show vs tell review** — does the lyric demonstrate emotion through image and action, or state it directly? Declaration can be powerful at the right moment, but imagery earns it
- **Universal vs. generic check** — is this so tied to one literal, personal specific that it can't generalize, or so smoothed-out it says nothing? Distinct from Cliche scan (worn phrasing, in Core Assessment) and Authenticity review (constructed vs. real, in Core Assessment) — this checks whether the underlying idea sits at a level of specificity that can connect beyond the writer's exact situation
- **Balance of Abstract and Specific** — a whole-song check. For each part of the song: how specific is the writing, how abstract is it (two separate scales, each low to high), what do the specific details and the abstract lines do for the song, and is that the right choice and intensity for what the part has to do? Neither end is wrong in itself: a deliberate abstract chorus or a plain line can be exactly right. Complements Specificity probe (a line at a time) and Show vs tell review (how directly a feeling is stated); this looks across the song.
